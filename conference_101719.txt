Abstract—This paper studies quadruped robot navigation
under strict safety and eﬀiciency requirements. We formulate
the task as a minimum-cost reach-avoid problem, where the
robot must reach the goal without entering unsafe states while
minimizing cumulative execution cost. To make this formulation practical for quadruped systems, we propose HD-MCRA,
a hierarchical RC-PPO with data-driven conservative margins
that combines a frozen low-level robust velocity tracker with a
high-level budget-conditioned reach-avoid policy. Representative simulation results show that HD-MCRA achieves a better
balance among success rate, collision rate, budget violation
rate, minimum clearance, and navigation cost than the reachavoid baseline, the minimum-cost reach-avoid baseline, and
PPO/CPPO-based baselines.
Index Terms—quadruped robot, navigation, reach-avoid,
hierarchical reinforcement learning, safe reinforcement learning

I. Introduction
Recent advances in learning-based quadruped locomotion have substantially improved agile motion and terrain
adaptation on physical robots [1]–[3]. As a result, the main
bottleneck for deployment has increasingly shifted from
low-level locomotion capability to high-level autonomous
navigation in cluttered environments. In practical missions
such as inspection, search and rescue, and security patrol,
quadruped navigation must satisfy two hard requirements
simultaneously: collision-free goal reaching and low execution cost. This is diﬀicult because navigation decisions
are tightly coupled with low-level locomotion, contact-rich
execution, and perception uncertainty.
Existing methods do not match this objective well.
Classical planners such as DWA and D* Lite remain
useful references [4], [5], but they usually rely on modeling
and execution assumptions that are hard to maintain
for contact-rich quadruped platforms. Most reinforcement
learning methods instead optimize weighted rewards or
constrained objectives [6]–[8], which softens safe goalreaching into a tunable preference or introduces thresholdselection sensitivity. For real quadruped deployment, however, the desired objective is not merely high average
reward, but guaranteed safe arrival with minimum cumulative cost.

A more suitable formulation is the minimum-cost reachavoid (MCRA) problem, where safe goal-reaching is enforced as a trajectory-level constraint while execution
cost is minimized. Recent augmented-state and budgetconditioned reach-avoid methods [9], [10] provide a principled bridge from safety-constrained planning to costaware policy learning. However, applying them directly to
quadruped navigation remains diﬀicult for two reasons:
they rely on deterministic closed-loop dynamics, while
robust quadruped deployment typically requires domain
randomization and residual execution uncertainty; and
they are mainly developed for simpler systems, whereas
practical quadruped navigation involves high-dimensional
perception and contact-rich locomotion.
To address these issues, we propose HD-MCRA
(Hierarchical Data-driven Minimum-Cost Reach-Avoid),
a hierarchical RC-PPO with data-driven conservative
margins for quadruped navigation. We first train a lowlevel robust velocity tracker with PPO under domain
randomization and then freeze it to induce an approximately deterministic interface for high-level learning.
On top of this interface, we learn a high-level budgetconditioned reach-avoid policy over low-dimensional velocity commands and introduce two data-driven conservative
margins, α and δ, to compensate for budget overrun and
trajectory deviation. The main contributions are twofold:
1) introducing the MCRA formulation into quadruped
navigation to explicitly encode safe goal-reaching with
cost minimization; and 2) developing a hierarchical RCPPO with data-driven conservative margins that makes
budget-conditioned MCRA policy learning practical for
quadruped robots.

III. HD-MCRA
A. Framework Overview
To make minimum-cost reach-avoid learning applicable
to quadruped robots, we propose HD-MCRA, a hierarchical RC-PPO with data-driven conservative margins.
The key idea is to decompose the original end-to-end
problem into two coupled but functionally distinct layers:
a high-level navigation policy that reasons about safe and
eﬀicient motion in the reach-avoid sense, and a low-level
locomotion controller that robustly tracks commanded
base velocities. This decomposition reduces the learning
diﬀiculty caused by high-dimensional joint-level control
and allows the high-level policy to focus on navigation semantics rather than contact-rich whole-body stabilization.
In particular, the high-level budget-conditioned policy
design follows the same MCRA semantics emphasized by
the minimum-cost reach-avoid baseline (RC-PPO) [10],
while adapting it to a hierarchical quadruped setting.
The overall training pipeline is illustrated by two stages.
In the first stage, a low-level policy is trained in simulation
to track desired body velocity commands under domain
randomization. The purpose of this stage is not to solve
navigation directly, but to provide a robust motion interface that maps commanded base velocities to stable
quadruped locomotion. In the second stage, the trained
low-level policy is frozen and embedded into the highlevel environment. The high-level policy then receives local
perception and task-related observations, together with a

cost budget variable, and outputs desired base velocity
commands. Since the low-level controller is fixed, the highlevel learner interacts with an approximately stationary
closed-loop system, which is much more suitable for
budget-conditioned reach-avoid learning.

D. Data-driven Conservative Margins for Sim-to-Real Robustness
Although the frozen low-level controller provides a more
stable interface, real quadruped deployment still exhibits
residual mismatch between simulated and realized trajectories. Two typical failure modes are especially important.
First, due to tracking delay or locomotion disturbance, the
actual execution time may exceed the planned budget,
causing a nominally feasible trajectory to violate the
cost constraint. Second, due to body oscillation or lateral
deviation, the realized robot trajectory may pass closer to
obstacles than expected, causing collision even when the
nominal high-level plan is safe.

hierarchical control decomposition, budget-conditioned
reach-avoid learning, and uncertainty-aware conservative
correction, making the MCRA formulation practically
deployable on quadruped robots.
IV. Experiments and Results
A. Experimental Setup
We evaluate the proposed HD-MCRA framework on
representative quadruped navigation tasks in cluttered environments. Following the problem formulation in Section
II, the robot receives local obstacle observations together
with goal-relative information and outputs commanded
body velocities to safely reach the target while minimizing
cumulative navigation cost. To cover different levels of
navigation diﬀiculty, we consider three types of scenarios:
1) moderately cluttered scenes with multiple feasible
routes, 2) narrow-passage scenes that require precise
obstacle avoidance, and 3) densely obstructed scenes where
safety–eﬀiciency trade-offs are more pronounced.

D. Ablation on Hierarchy and Conservative Margins
To further understand the role of each design component, we conduct ablation studies on the hierarchical
structure and the two conservative margins. The compared
variants include: 1) the full HD-MCRA framework, 2) a
version without hierarchical decomposition (w/o Hierarchy), 3) a version without the cost margin α, 4) a version
without the safety margin δ, and 5) an RC-PPO-style
without conservative margins variant, which removes both
margins while keeping the hierarchical budget-conditioned
policy.
As shown in Table III, removing the hierarchical structure causes a clear performance drop, with the success
rate decreasing from 70.8% to 60.1%, the average cost
increasing from 71.3 to 95.8, and the minimum clearance
shrinking from 0.32 m to 0.18 m. This result supports the
main architectural motivation of HD-MCRA: by assigning locomotion tracking to the low-level controller and
navigation reasoning to the high-level policy, the learning
problem becomes substantially easier and more stable
than direct end-to-end decision making.
The two conservative margins also play distinct roles.
Removing the cost margin α mainly degrades budget

feasibility, as reflected by the increase of BVR from
11.2% to 21.4%, while the minimum clearance remains
relatively close to the full model. In contrast, removing
the safety margin δ has a stronger impact on collisionrelated performance, increasing CR from 18.6% to 25.8%
and reducing MC from 0.32 m to 0.19 m. The RC-PPOstyle variant without conservative margins performs worst
among the hierarchical variants, showing that the two
mechanisms are complementary rather than redundant.
Overall, the results in this section support the main
claim of the paper: HD-MCRA improves quadruped navigation by combining the semantics of the minimumcost reach-avoid baseline with a hierarchical quadruped
interface and data-driven conservative margins. Compared
with the reach-avoid baseline, the minimum-cost reachavoid baseline, Reward Shaping, and CPPO, the proposed
framework achieves a better balance among success, safety,
budget feasibility, and eﬀiciency.
V. Conclusion
This paper formulated quadruped navigation as a
minimum-cost reach-avoid problem and proposed HDMCRA, a hierarchical RC-PPO with data-driven conservative margins. By combining a frozen low-level robust
velocity tracker, a budget-conditioned high-level policy,
and conservative corrections for cost and safety, HDMCRA improves the balance among success, safety,
budget feasibility, and eﬀiciency relative to the reachavoid baseline, the minimum-cost reach-avoid baseline,
and PPO/CPPO baselines. The ablation study further
confirms that hierarchy mainly improves success and cost,
while the two conservative margins are responsible for
the gains in budget feasibility and safety margin. Future
work will extend the framework to more challenging
environments and more realistic deployment uncertainty.
