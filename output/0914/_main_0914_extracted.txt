Abstract: This paper studies the engineering of minimum-cost reach-avoid1
control for a real quadruped robot. We start from the RC-PPO formulation2
and identify the execution mismatch that appears when its idealized transi-3
tion model is connected to a learned locomotion controller. We then develop4
a deployment-oriented interface, data-calibrated cost and safety margins,5
and a budget-search procedure that together make the formulation usable6
on hardware. Simulation and physical-robot experiments evaluate each en-7
gineering module and the resulting navigation behavior. This version is8
a structure-review draft: all numerical values in the body are explicitly9
marked as assumed data and are not experimental evidence.10
Keywords: quadruped navigation, reach-avoid control, safe robot learning11
1 Introduction12
Quadruped robots are increasingly used for inspection, exploration, and service tasks in13
environments where a navigation policy must do more than reach a target. The robot14
must remain collision-free during the whole maneuver while avoiding unnecessary motion15
and energy expenditure. These requirements are coupled in cluttered scenes: a short route16
may pass close to obstacles, whereas a safer route may consume more time and actuation.17
Treating safety and eﬀiciency as independent reward terms therefore makes the desired18
trade-off dependent on hand-tuned weights. Minimum-cost reach-avoid (MCRA) control19
provides a more direct task description by requiring goal reachability and trajectory-wide20
avoidance while minimizing a cumulative cost [ 1, 2].21
Recent work has made MCRA learning practical through RC-PPO [ 3]. RC-PPO augments22
the state with a safety indicator and remaining budget, learns a reachability value function23
with a budget-conditioned policy, and then searches for the least feasible initial budget.24
These elements are important for quadruped navigation because they express cost feasibility25
and safety in the same trajectory objective. They are also the starting point of this paper;26
we do not introduce a new MCRA objective, augmented-state construction, or reachability-27
learning theorem.28
The diﬀiculty appears when the idealized MCRA model is connected to a real quadruped.29
A high-level action is usually a desired body velocity, not a directly applied state transition.30
It is realized by a locomotion controller whose tracking error depends on contacts, friction,31
latency, and sensing. Consequently, a nominal transition used during high-level learning can32
disagree with the executed motion in two task-relevant ways. First, the executed path may33
be longer or slower than the nominal path, so the nominal budget update underestimates34
cumulative cost. Second, the executed body path may deviate toward an obstacle, so a35
trajectory that is safe in the nominal map may lose its clearance on hardware. These are36

We present HD-MCRA, a deployment-oriented engineering layer for RC-PPO/MCRA on a39
quadruped robot. The design follows the failure modes above. A velocity-level interface is40
created by training a locomotion tracker once and freezing it before high-level learning. This41
interface reduces the navigation action space and, more importantly, defines a repeatable42
closed-loop mapping whose residual error can be measured. We collect paired nominal43
and executed rollouts through that mapping and use the resulting error distributions to44
calibrate two separate corrections. A cost margin α inflates the running cost used by the45
budget update, while a safety margin δ enlarges the unsafe set used by reach-avoid learning.46
The same budget-conditioned policy and two-phase search inherited from RC-PPO are then47
applied to this calibrated model.48
The engineering contribution is therefore the deployment pipeline that connects an ideal49
MCRA learner to a physical closed loop. It is not the low-level PPO tracker, which is50
a system dependency, nor a claim of strict stochastic safety. Our claims are limited to51
empirical robustness under the distribution used to calibrate the margins. We evaluate the52
pipeline with controlled mismatch studies, component-wise ablations, sensitivity tests, and53
a quantitative physical-robot deployment study. The experiments are designed to answer54
where direct RC-PPO deployment fails, whether the frozen interface makes the high-level55
problem repeatable, whether α and δ address different failure modes, and how the complete56
system behaves on hardware.57
Our contributions are:58
1. We identify the model–execution mismatch that must be handled when an MCRA59
policy is deployed through a quadruped locomotion controller, separating cost over-60
run from geometric path deviation.61
2. We develop an execution-calibrated deployment layer consisting of a frozen velocity62
interface, paired mismatch data, and two corrections that map measured cost and63
path errors to budget inflation ( α) and unsafe-set dilation ( δ).64
3. We evaluate the resulting RC-PPO-based system through controlled studies and65
physical-robot trials, with the protocol and claim boundaries made explicit for each66
module.67
The remainder of the paper first positions HD-MCRA relative to MCRA learning, quadruped68
navigation, and risk-aware planning. We then define the ideal MCRA problem and its69
closed-loop deployment gap, describe the execution-calibrated system, and evaluate each70
engineering choice before discussing limitations.71
2 Related Work72
2.1 Minimum-Cost Reach-A void Learning73
Reach-avoid reinforcement learning treats reaching a target and avoiding an unsafe set as74
trajectory-level requirements rather than competing reward terms [ 1, 4]. RC-PPO extends75
this view to minimum-cost reach-avoid control by augmenting the state with a safety in-76
dicator and remaining budget, learning a budget-conditioned policy and reachability critic,77
and searching for the least feasible initial budget [ 3]. This construction is the algorith-78
mic foundation used here. Our focus is the interface between its transition model and a79
quadruped’s executed motion, which is outside the deterministic model assumed by the80
original formulation.81

2.2 Safe Quadruped Navigation82
Learning-based quadruped systems have demonstrated robust locomotion and navigation83
over challenging terrain [ 5, 6, 7, 8, 9]. Recent navigation systems improve agility, percep-84
tion or traversal in confined and unstructured environments [ 10, 11, 12]. Safety is also ad-85
dressed through constrained policy optimization, control barrier functions, and risk-sensitive86
objectives [13, 14, 15]. These methods establish useful hardware interfaces and safety mech-87
anisms, but they do not by themselves calibrate a minimum-cost reach-avoid budget and an88
avoid-set boundary from the same execution data.89
2.3 Robust Deployment under Model–Execution Mismatch90
Domain randomization and robust reinforcement learning reduce sensitivity to model un-91
certainty [ 16, 17, 18, 19], while chance- constrained and risk-aware planners represent un-92
certainty explicitly in their traversability or path-selection models [ 20, 21, 22]. These ap-93
proaches differ from the present deployment layer in both object and mechanism. STEP94
estimates stochastic traversability for risk-aware off-road planning, and Schmittle et al. use95
multiple long-range samples under sensing uncertainty; HD-MCRA instead measures paired96
nominal/executed errors after fixing a velocity interface, then maps upper empirical quan-97
tiles to the MCRA running cost and unsafe set. The resulting claim is correspondingly98
narrower: empirical robustness under the calibration distribution, without a new formal99
stochastic guarantee.100
The remaining gap is thus practical rather than theoretical: an MCRA learner needs a101
repeatable closed-loop interface and an explicit procedure for converting its measured ex-102
ecution mismatch into the two quantities that determine feasibility, namely cumulative103
budget and geometric clearance.104

3.3 Deployment Objective and Scope131
The deployment objective is to retain the RC-PPO MCRA semantics while accounting for132
the measured distribution of (εc, εd). HD-MCRA does not extend the deterministic equiva-133
lence theorem of RC-PPO to a general stochastic system. Instead, it constructs an empirical134
calibration layer and evaluates whether the resulting policy remains safe and budget-feasible135
under the calibration and held-out execution conditions. This scope determines the method136
and experiment design in the next sections.137

5.6 RQ5: Physical-Robot Deployment268
The draft physical study uses a Unitree Go2 in a cluttered indoor environment with chairs,269
narrow passages and a moving obstacle. The representative route length is approximately 15270
m and the narrow passage is about 0.3 m wider than the robot body (assumed data, inherited271
from the 0820 description). The assumed deployment count is 26 successful trials out of 30,272
corresponding to SR 86.7% (assumed data). The remaining four trials are provisionally273
classified as two localization failures, one conservative timeout and one contact with a chair274
leg (assumed data). This classification must be replaced by the actual trial log. We report275
the deployment as an engineering study, not as a statistical certification.276
6 Limitations277
The current manuscript is an engineering draft and has several limitations. First, the nu-278
merical values in this version are explicitly assumed placeholders; no result should be used279
for a scientific comparison until replaced by trial-level logs and confidence intervals. Second,280

the robustness mechanism uses fixed empirical quantiles and therefore only describes cover-281
age under the calibration distribution. Temporal correlation, rare contacts and distribution282
shifts can make the effective coverage lower than the marginal quantile suggests. Third, a283
fixed α may be overly conservative in open space and insuﬀicient under severe actuator or284
perception faults, while a fixed δ can remove feasible narrow passages. Fourth, the LiDAR285
observation can miss thin structures or obstacles behind occlusions. Finally, the current286
physical study is small and indoor; broader terrain, payload and weather conditions are287
required before making claims about general quadruped deployment.288
7 Conclusion289
This paper reframes the practical use of RC-PPO/MCRA on a quadruped as a deployment290
problem. The MCRA objective, augmented state and two-phase solver are inherited from291
prior work. The new engineering layer fixes a velocity-level closed-loop interface, measures292
paired nominal/executed mismatch, and maps cost overrun and path deviation to separate293
corrections α and δ. This organization turns an abstract budget-conditioned reach-avoid294
learner into a pipeline that can be calibrated, tested and deployed on a physical robot. The295
present numerical results are placeholders marked as assumed data; the final conclusions296
will be updated after replacing them with verified simulation and Go2 logs. The intended297
claim is empirical robustness within the calibrated execution distribution, not a new formal298
stochastic reach-avoid guarantee.299
