Abstract: Quadruped navigation in cluttered environments requires reaching tar-1
get locations, avoiding unsafe regions throughout execution, and minimizing cu-2
mulative cost. We formulate this task as a minimum-cost reach-avoid problem,3
which explicitly encodes goal reaching, safety preservation, and cost minimiza-4
tion within a unified trajectory-level objective. To make this formulation practi-5
cal for quadruped robots, we propose HD-MCRA, a hierarchical framework that6
combines a frozen robust low-level velocity tracker with a budget-conditioned7
high-level reach-avoid policy. The low-level tracker induces a stable closed-loop8
abstraction for high-level planning, while data-driven conservative margins im-9
prove robustness in both budget feasibility and safety. Simulation and real-world10
experiments show that HD-MCRA achieves a better trade-off among task suc-11
cess, safety, budget feasibility, clearance, and navigation cost than representative12
baselines.13

1 Introduction16
Recent advances in learning-based quadruped locomotion and agile navigation have substantially17
improved motion capability and terrain adaptation on physical robots [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6]. As a result,18
the primary deployment bottleneck has shifted from low-level locomotion capability to high-level19
autonomous navigation in cluttered environments [7, 8, 9]. In practical missions such as inspection,20
search and rescue, and security patrol, quadruped navigation must satisfy two hard requirements21
simultaneously: collision-free goal reaching and low execution cost under contact-rich execution22
and perception uncertainty.23
Classical planners such as Dynamic Window Approach (DW A) and D* Lite [10, 11, 12] have pro-24
vided effective foundations for collision avoidance and goal-directed navigation. Reinforcement25
learning approaches have further advanced safe navigation through weighted rewards and con-26
strained objectives [13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 18]. Recent reachability-based methods encode safety, live-27
ness, and cost feasibility beyond additive rewards, including reach-avoid RL and minimum-cost28
reach-avoid (MCRA) formulations such as budget-conditioned RC-PPO [19, 20, 21, 22, 23]. How-29
ever, existing methods do not fully address the quadruped navigation problem: classical planners30
rely on assumptions that are difficult to maintain on quadruped platforms; RL approaches either re-31
lax safe goal-reaching into a tunable preference or are sensitive to constraint thresholds; and reach-32
ability formulations are typically developed under deterministic or simplified dynamics, whereas33
quadruped deployment still faces stochastic execution mismatches.34
To overcome these limitations, HD-MCRA (Hierarchical Data-driven Minimum-Cost Reach-35
Avoid) is proposed as a hierarchical MCRA framework with data-driven conservative margins for36
quadruped navigation. A robust low-level velocity tracker is first trained with PPO under domain37

3.3 Data-driven Robustness Margins134
Despite the concentrated closed-loop abstraction, residual mismatch remains during real-world135
quadruped execution [29, 30, 31]. This mismatch mainly manifests as cost overruns and trajectory136
deviations, which can respectively violate budget feasibility and reduce safety margins.137

budget robustness and a safety margin δ for geometric robustness.139

4 Experiments167
4.1 Experimental Setup168
Experiments were conducted on quadruped navigation tasks in cluttered environments, with three169
levels of difficulty: moderately cluttered scenes, narrow passages, and densely obstructed scenes.170
We compare HD-MCRA with RA [20], RC-PPO [21], reward-shaped PPO, and CPPO with two171
safety weights (βL, βH). For a controlled comparison, all methods were evaluated using the same172
high-level observations, commanded-velocity action interface, frozen low-level tracker, and train-173
ing/evaluation maps [13, 14, 18, 34, 35]. Performance is measured by Success Rate (SR), Collision174
Rate (CR), Average Cost, Budget Violation Rate (BVR), and Minimum Clearance (MC).175
4.2 Baseline Comparison176
Figure 2a shows the scenario-wise comparison in terms of SR, CR, Average Cost, and MC. Table 1177
summarizes the average performance over the three scenarios and also reports BVR.178
Figure 2a and Table 1 indicate that HD-MCRA achieves the most balanced overall performance179
across task completion, safety, budget feasibility, and efficiency in the three scenarios. Relative to180
RA, HD-MCRA achieves a higher success rate and lower cost while maintaining comparable safety.181
Compared with RC-PPO, HD-MCRA further improves budget feasibility and slightly increases min-182
imum clearance, while also attaining the best average success rate. The PPO/CPPO baselines exhibit183
the usual trade-off: some settings remain competitive in easier scenes or improve clearance, but none184
matches HD-MCRA in terms of success, budget feasibility, and efficiency simultaneously.185

4.5 Physical Robot Experiments206
The learned HD-MCRA policy was deployed on a Unitree Go2 robot in a cluttered indoor envi-207
ronment containing chairs, narrow passages, and moving obstacles. In one representative setup, a208
chair-defined passage was created whose width was only about 0.3 m larger than the robot. The209
chairs were placed in an alternating left-front, right-front, and left-front pattern, requiring the robot210
to execute consecutive turning maneuvers. A moving obstacle was further introduced near the goal211
region to test reactive avoidance under real-world execution mismatch.212

5 Limitations221
While HD-MCRA improves the trade-off among success, safety, budget feasibility, and efficiency222
in the considered quadruped navigation tasks, several limitations remain. First, its robustness mech-223
anism is based on a nominal closed-loop abstraction and empirical quantile corrections rather than a224
strict stochastic MCRA guarantee. As a result, the conservative marginsα and δ may become insuf-225
ficient when the execution distribution deviates substantially from the domain-randomized training226
conditions. Second, the current system focuses on navigation policy design and relies on LiDAR-227
based observations, which may provide limited geometric saliency for thin structures such as chair228
legs, especially under partial occlusion or small clearances. Future work will explore more princi-229
pled stochastic reachability formulations, uncertainty-aware adaptive safety margins, and stronger230
multimodal perception for geometry-sensitive safety.231
6 Conclusion232
In this paper, we formulated quadruped navigation as an MCRA problem and proposed HD-MCRA,233
which combines a frozen low-level tracker, a nominal closed-loop abstraction, a budget-conditioned234
high-level policy, and conservative cost and safety corrections. This design makes augmented-state235
MCRA practical for quadruped navigation and achieves a better trade-off among success, safety,236
budget feasibility, and efficiency than the evaluated baselines.237
