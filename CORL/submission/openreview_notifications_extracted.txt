[CoRL 2026] Clarification on the Post-Rebuttal Review Process
Hide Message
Dear Authors,
We would like to clarify the next steps in the CoRL 2026 review process following the rebuttal
deadline.
There is no author–reviewer discussion period. Authors should not use the OpenReview
message/comment box to contact reviewers, Area Chairs, or Senior Area Chairs to
provide additional responses or materials after the rebuttal deadline.
We would also like to clarify that authors of papers that received a first-round rejection
should not submit a rebuttal. The rebuttal stage applies only to submissions that were
explicitly advanced to rebuttal.
The review process has now moved to the internal post-rebuttal stage. Reviewers will read the
rebuttals, discuss the submissions with their Area Chairs, and complete their post-rebuttal
assessments. Area Chairs, Senior Area Chairs, and Program Chairs will subsequently finalize
the recommendations and decisions.
Some post-rebuttal reviewer updates may currently be visible to authors due to an
OpenReview configuration issue. These are intended to be visible only to the reviewing
committees at this stage and will be hidden from authors. Please do not respond to them
or take any action based on them.
At this point, there are no further tasks for authors. Please wait for the final acceptance
notifications, which will be released on September 4, 2026.
Thank you for your understanding and cooperation.
Best regards,
The CoRL 2026 Program Chairs

Dear CoRL 2026 Authors,
We are writing with a reminder about the review timeline and the rebuttal process,
particularly as many of you may be traveling or on vacation in early August.
Timeline
August 4: Reviews are released to authors on OpenReview.
August 4–11: Rebuttal period. The submission deadline is August 11, 2026, 11:59 PM
(AoE). This window is one week and will not be extended, so please plan availability
accordingly.
Decision structure this year CoRL 2026 implements a two-stage decision process. A subset
of papers will receive a first-round rejection together with their reviews on August 4; these
papers do not proceed to the rebuttal phase. All remaining papers advance to the rebuttal
period, and final decisions will be made after the rebuttal is considered by the Area Chairs and
Senior Area Chairs.
Rebuttal format (please read carefully)
Use the official one-page rebuttal template linked on the conference website
(Contributions → Instruction for Rebuttal). All content, including references, must fit on
one page, with unchanged font size and spacing. Rebuttals that do not adhere to the
template will be desk rejected.
The rebuttal must remain anonymous and must not include external links (videos, code,
websites).
The purpose of the rebuttal is to correct factual inaccuracies in the reviews and to provide
clarifications or additional details specifically requested by the reviewers.
You will not be able to respond to individual reviewers on OpenReview, and papers cannot
be updated during the rebuttal period. Accepted papers will be able to incorporate
reviewer and Area Chair feedback in the camera-ready version via an additional page.
To submit, click the “Rebuttal” button on your paper’s OpenReview page, upload your PDF,
and submit the form.
We thank you for your contributions to CoRL 2026 and your patience during the review
process, and we look forward to seeing you in Austin, November 9–12.
Best regards, The CoRL 2026 Program Chairs

Title: Hierarchical Minimum-Cost Reach-Avoid Learning for Safe Quadruped Navigation
Abstract Quadruped navigation in cluttered environments requires reaching target locations,
avoiding unsafe regions throughout execution, and minimizing cumulative cost. We formulate
this task as a minimum-cost reach-avoid problem, which explicitly encodes goal reaching,
safety preservation, and cost minimization within a unified trajectory-level objective. To make
this formulation practical for quadruped robots, we propose HD-MCRA, a hierarchical
framework that combines a frozen robust low-level velocity tracker with a budget-conditioned
high-level reach-avoid policy. The low-level tracker induces a stable closed-loop abstraction for
high-level planning, while data-driven conservative margins improve robustness in both
budget feasibility and safety. Simulation and real-world experiments show that HD-MCRA
achieves a better trade-off among task success, safety, budget feasibility, clearance, and
navigation cost than representative baselines.
