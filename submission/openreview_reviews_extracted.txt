Abstract: Quadruped navigation in cluttered environments requires reaching target locations, avoiding unsafe regions
throughout execution, and minimizing cumulative cost. We formulate this task as a minimum-cost reach-avoid problem,
which explicitly encodes goal reaching, safety preservation, and cost minimization within a unified trajectory-level
objective. To make this formulation practical for quadruped robots, we propose HD-MCRA, a hierarchical framework that
combines a frozen robust low-level velocity tracker with a budget-conditioned high-level reach-avoid policy. The low-level
tracker induces a stable closed-loop abstraction for high-level planning, while data-driven conservative margins improve
robustness in both budget feasibility and safety. Simulation and real-world experiments show that HD-MCRA achieves a
better trade-off among task success, safety, budget feasibility, clearance, and navigation cost than representative
baselines.

Summary:
In this paper, the authors introduce a hierarchical RL-based controller for quadruped navigation built around a
reach-avoid framework. In order to reduce the complexity of learning a controller using the full quadruped
dynamics, a high-level RL controller designed to solve the overall minimum-cost reach-avoid problem sends
commands to a low-level controller designed to track the high-level command. The high-level reach-avoid
controller uses the low-level closed-loop dynamics, along with stochasticity from domain randomization, as part
of its probabilistic transition map. In order to robustify the interconnection between the high and low-level
controllers, margins on the cost budget and unsafe set are introduced so that the overall system remains safe.
Strengths:
The paper’s idea to simplify the problem of quadruped navigation by making it hierarchical is very interesting and
compelling, as well as its recognition of the fact that such a hierarchical control architecture may require data-
driven margins to make the interconnection between the controllers robust. The overall problem is relevant to the
literature. Also, Figure 1 very nicely encapsulates the proposed method.

Summary:
The work proposes a hierarchical reinforcement learning-based navigation framework for quadrupeds, focusing
on collision-free reaching while minimizing execution cost. The key contribution of the work relates to data-driven
safety and cost margins being used by the high-level policy to account for execution time mismatch of the low-
level policy due to stochasticity resulting from the contact-rich nature of quadruped locomotion.
Strengths:
1. The authors identify that considering cost and safety margins when training the high-level policy is useful for
practical performance.
2. The results appear promising
Weaknesses:
1. As I understand, the key contribution of this work relates to understanding cost and safety margins to handle
execution time stochasticity. Assuming this is the case, it should be the center of the reader’s attention. Right
now, it appears on page 5.
2. Assuming that the key challenge is stochasticity at execution time, the derivation of the proposed formulation
is unclear. As written, the extra cost and safety margins are introduced somewhat arbitrarily. As an example,
uncertainty has been handled in planning from a risk minimization perspective[1,2]. A stronger discussion
comparing the proposed formulation against these alternatives would help establish that the method is
addressing a fundamental gap rather than introducing one possible heuristic treatment of execution-time
variability.
3. In the introduction: “existing methods do not fully address the quadruped navigation problem: classical
planners rely on assumptions that are difficult to maintain on quadruped platforms” – please Specify what
assumptions are being referred to, and possibly provide citations for this.
4. None of the results appear to have confidence intervals, which makes statistical significance hard to gauge.
Please provide the confidence intervals.
5. Assuming the central motivation of the paper is handling execution-time stochasticity of the low-level policy
when used by a higher-level controller, the real-world experimental evaluation does not appear to highlight
this aspect sufficiently. The presented environments are relatively structured, and it is unclear whether they
exhibit the type of execution time uncertainty that motivates the paper. Experiments in more representative
or challenging environments would strengthen the empirical validation.

Summary:
The paper proposes HD-MCRA, a hierarchical method for safe quadruped navigation in cluttered environments. It
formulates navigation as a minimum-cost reach-avoid problem, where the robot must reach the goal, avoid
unsafe regions, and minimize execution cost. The method uses a frozen low-level velocity tracker and a budget-
conditioned high-level policy, with empirical cost and safety margins to improve robustness. The authors evaluate
the approach in simulation against several safe navigation baselines and also report deployment results on a
Unitree Go2 robot.
Strengths:
1. The problem formulation is meaningful. Modeling quadruped navigation as a minimum-cost reach-avoid
problem is a principled way to combine goal reaching, safety, and cost minimization.
2. The hierarchical design is reasonable. Using a frozen low-level velocity tracker and learning a budget-
conditioned high-level policy makes the reach-avoid problem more practical for a quadruped robot.
3. The experimental validation is fairly complete. The paper includes comparisons with several relevant
baselines, budget-feasibility analysis, ablations on the hierarchy and conservative margins, and real-world
deployment on a Unitree Go2.
Weaknesses:
1. The technical novelty is somewhat incremental. The paper combines several existing ideas, including
hierarchical velocity-command control, reach-avoid learning, budget conditioning, and empirical conservative
margins. While the combination is useful, the paper should make clearer what the main new algorithmic
contribution is beyond adapting these components to quadruped navigation.
2. Some key technical details are under-specified. The paper emphasizes minimum cost and budget feasibility,
but the exact cost function used in experiments is not clearly described. The high-level learning procedure is
also not fully specified, including the actor objective, optimization details, and terminal handling. These
details are important for assessing and reproducing the method.
3. The safety and robustness claims should be stated more carefully. The conservative margins are estimated
from empirical quantiles, so they improve robustness under the sampled execution distribution but do not
provide a strict stochastic reach-avoid guarantee. The paper should avoid implying stronger safety
guarantees than the method actually supports.

Limitations And Broader Impact:
The paper includes a limitations section and acknowledges important issues, including that the robustness
margins are empirical rather than strict stochastic reach-avoid guarantees, and that LiDAR-based observations
may be insufficient for thin structures or partially occluded objects.

scope of the real-world evaluation and the remaining uncertainty about robustness outside the tested indoor
environments. Second, since the method is intended for safe quadruped navigation, potential failure cases in
