\begin{abstract}
Quadruped navigation in cluttered environments requires reaching target locations, avoiding unsafe regions throughout execution, and minimizing cumulative cost. We formulate this task as a minimum-cost reach-avoid problem, which explicitly encodes goal reaching, safety preservation, and cost minimization within a unified trajectory-level objective. To make this formulation practical for quadruped robots, we propose HD-MCRA, a hierarchical framework that combines a frozen robust low-level velocity tracker with a budget-conditioned high-level reach-avoid policy. The low-level tracker induces a stable closed-loop abstraction for high-level planning, while data-driven conservative margins improve robustness in both budget feasibility and safety. Simulation and real-world experiments show that HD-MCRA achieves a better trade-off among task success, safety, budget feasibility, clearance, and navigation cost than representative baselines.
\end{abstract}

\keywords{robot navigation and locomotion, robot safety, reach-avoid reinforcement learning}

\section{Introduction}

Recent advances in learning-based quadruped locomotion and agile navigation have substantially improved motion capability and terrain adaptation on physical robots~\citep{hwangbo2019,lee2020,kumar2021,miki2022,hoeller2024,cheng2024}. As a result, the primary deployment bottleneck has shifted from low-level locomotion capability to high-level autonomous navigation in cluttered environments~\citep{chen2026,zhang2024,elnoor2024}. In practical missions such as inspection, search and rescue, and security patrol, quadruped navigation must satisfy two hard requirements simultaneously: collision-free goal reaching and low execution cost under contact-rich execution and perception uncertainty.

Classical planners such as Dynamic Window Approach (DWA) and D* Lite~\citep{fox1997,koenig2002,karaman2011} have provided effective foundations for collision avoidance and goal-directed navigation. Reinforcement learning approaches have further advanced safe navigation through weighted rewards and constrained objectives~\citep{achiam2017,tessler2019,liu2022cvpo,garcia2015,wachi2024,guerrier2024}. Recent reachability-based methods encode safety, liveness, and cost feasibility beyond additive rewards, including reach-avoid RL and minimum-cost reach-avoid (MCRA) formulations such as budget-conditioned RC-PPO~\citep{bansal2017,hsu2021,so2024,ganai2024survey,ganai2023}. However, existing methods do not fully address the quadruped navigation problem: classical planners rely on assumptions that are difficult to maintain on quadruped platforms; RL approaches either relax safe goal-reaching into a tunable preference or are sensitive to constraint thresholds; and reachability formulations are typically developed under deterministic or simplified dynamics, whereas quadruped deployment still faces stochastic execution mismatches.

To overcome these limitations, HD-MCRA (Hierarchical Data-driven Minimum-Cost Reach-Avoid) is proposed as a hierarchical MCRA framework with data-driven conservative margins for quadruped navigation. A robust low-level velocity tracker is first trained with PPO under domain randomization and then frozen to induce an approximately deterministic interface for high-level learning. Building on this interface, a high-level budget-conditioned reach-avoid policy is learned over low-dimensional velocity commands, and two data-driven conservative margins, $\alpha$ and $\delta$, are introduced to compensate for budget overrun and trajectory deviation.

The main contributions are twofold: 1) quadruped navigation is formulated as an MCRA problem that explicitly encodes safe goal-reaching and cost minimization; and 2) a hierarchical MCRA framework with data-driven conservative margins is developed to make budget-conditioned MCRA learning practical for quadruped robots.

The paper is organized as follows. Section~2 introduces the MCRA formulation for quadruped navigation. Section~3 presents the HD-MCRA framework and its learning procedure. Section~4 evaluates the proposed method in simulation and real-world experiments. Section~5 discusses the main limitations of the current study. Section~6 concludes the paper.

\section{Problem Formulation}
% TASK-006：式 (1)–(12)
To formalize quadruped navigation in cluttered environments, the high-level decision-making process is modeled as a Markov decision process (MDP)
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{M}=\langle \mathcal{X}, \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{high}}, P, c, \rho_0 \rangle
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{X}$ denotes the high-level state space, $\mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{high}}$ denotes the action space of commanded base velocities, $P(\cdot|x_t,u_t)$ denotes the transition kernel, $c(x_t,u_t)$ denotes the instantaneous execution cost, and $\rho_0$ denotes the initial-state distribution.

Let $\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{X}$ denote the goal set and $\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{X}$ denote the unsafe set induced by collision or safety violation. Starting from an initial state $x_0$, the task is to find a policy $\pi$ and a free terminal time $T$ such that the robot reaches $\mathcal{G}$, avoids $\mathcal{F}$ throughout the trajectory, and minimizes the cumulative cost. Motivated by the deterministic MCRA formulation in~\citep{so2024}, a stochastic MCRA problem is considered for quadruped navigation~\citep{bansal2017}:
\begin{align}
\min_{\pi,T}\quad & \sum_{t=0}^{T-1} c\!\left(x_t,\pi(x_t)\right) \label{eq:mcra_obj}\\
\mathrm{s.t.}\quad & x_T \in \mathcal{G}, \label{eq:mcra_goal}\\
& x_t \notin \mathcal{F},\quad \forall t\in\{0,\dots,T\}, \label{eq:mcra_safe}\\
& x_{t+1}\sim P\!\left(\cdot\mid x_t,\pi(x_t)\right). \label{eq:mcra_dyn}
\end{align}
The goal and unsafe sets are represented by two signed functions
$g:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$ and $h:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}$ as
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{G} := \{x\in\mathcal{X}\mid g(x)\le 0\}, \qquad
\mathcal{F} := \{x\in\mathcal{X}\mid h(x)>0\}
\end{equation}
Unlike reward shaping, this formulation enforces goal-reaching and safety as trajectory-level constraints rather than soft preferences induced by weighted rewards. This distinction is important in quadruped navigation, where surrogate reward or CMDP objectives may not align with the actual objective of safe goal-reaching with minimum cumulative cost~\citep{so2024}.

RC-PPO recasts the MCRA problem as a reachability problem on an augmented state space~\citep{so2024}. This construction was developed for deterministic discrete-time dynamics of the form $x_{t+1}=f(x_t,u_t)$, rather than for the stochastic dynamics in~\eqref{eq:mcra_dyn}. The deterministic augmented-state formulation is therefore briefly recalled below.

The augmented state is defined as
\begin{equation}
\hat{x}_t=(x_t,y_t,z_t)\in \hat{\mathcal{X}}:=\mathcal{X}\times\{-1,1\}\times\mathbb{R}
\end{equation}
where $y_t$ is a binary safety indicator and $z_t$ is a remaining cost budget. The corresponding augmented dynamics are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat{f}(x_t,y_t,z_t,u_t)
= \big(&f(x_t,u_t), \\
&\max\{I_{f(x_t,u_t)\in\mathcal{F}},y_t\}, \\
&z_t-c(x_t,u_t)\big)
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $I_{b\in\mathcal{B}}$ is the shifted indicator. By construction, $z_t$ evolves as
\begin{equation}
z_{t+1}=z_0-\sum_{k=0}^{t} c(x_k,u_k)
\end{equation}
which means that $z_0$ serves as an upper bound on the cumulative cost incurred along the trajectory. Then the augmented goal function can be defined as
\begin{equation}
\hat{g}(x,y,z)=\max\{g(x),Cy,-z\}
\end{equation}
with augmented goal set
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\hat{\mathcal{G}}
&=\{\hat{x}\mid \hat{g}(\hat{x})\le 0\} \\
&=\{(x,y,z)\mid x\in\mathcal{G},\, y=-1,\, z\ge 0\}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
Under this construction, if the augmented state reaches the augmented goal set $\hat{\mathcal{G}}$, then the original system reaches the goal, remains safe along the whole trajectory, and does not exceed the prescribed budget~\citep{so2024}. Accordingly, the deterministic MCRA problem can be equivalently interpreted as searching for the least feasible initial budget
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\pi,z_0}\quad & z_0 \\
\mathrm{s.t.}\quad & \tilde{V}^{\pi}_{\hat{g}}(x_0,I_{x_0\in\mathcal{F}},z_0)\le 0,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{V}^{\pi}_{\hat{g}}$ is the reach value function on the augmented system.

However, this deterministic reformulation alone is insufficient for quadruped navigation, since real-world execution remains stochastic and can lead to budget overruns and trajectory deviations~\citep{bansal2020}. To bridge this gap, we propose HD-MCRA, a hierarchical MCRA framework with a quadruped-specific nominal closed-loop abstraction, budget-conditioned high-level policy learning, and data-driven conservative corrections.

\section{HD-MCRA}
% TASK-007 / TASK-008
\subsection{Framework Overview}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{../../images/framework_overview_final.png}
\caption{Overview of the HD-MCRA framework.}
\label{fig:framework_overview}
\end{figure}

As shown in Figure~\ref{fig:framework_overview}, HD-MCRA consists of a frozen low-level locomotion controller, a budget-conditioned high-level planner, and data-driven conservative margins. This hierarchical structure reduces the action dimensionality of the navigation problem, preserves locomotion feasibility, and provides a more stationary closed-loop system for high-level MCRA learning~\citep{jain2019,li2023}. The frozen low-level tracker serves as a stable motion interface, while the conservative margins $\alpha$ and $\delta$ further compensate for residual mismatch in cost and safety.

Formally, let the overall policy be written as
\begin{equation}
u_t^{\mathrm{joint}}
=\pi^{\mathrm{low}}\big(s_t^{\mathrm{low}},u_t^{\mathrm{high}}\big),\qquad
u_t^{\mathrm{high}}
=\pi^{\mathrm{high}}(o_t,z_t)
\end{equation}
where $o_t$ denotes the high-level observation, $u_t^{\mathrm{high}}$ denotes the commanded base velocity, $s_t^{\mathrm{low}}$ denotes the low-level locomotion state, and $u_t^{\mathrm{joint}}$ denotes the final joint-level control action. This factorization allows the navigation problem to be solved in a lower-dimensional action space while preserving the physical feasibility of locomotion execution.

\subsection{Hierarchical Budgeted Policy Learning}

The low-level controller maps proprioceptive observations to actuator commands to track high-level velocity commands while preserving locomotion stability. Its state is $s_t^{\mathrm{low}} \in \mathcal{S}^{\mathrm{low}}$, which includes joint positions, joint velocities, body orientation, angular velocity, and recent tracking information, and its action is the joint-level command $u_t^{\mathrm{joint}} \in \mathcal{U}^{\mathrm{joint}}$. Given a commanded base velocity
\begin{equation}
u_t^{\mathrm{high}} = \big[v_{x,t}^{\ast}, v_{y,t}^{\ast}, \omega_{z,t}^{\ast}\big]^{\top}
\end{equation}
the controller aims to minimize tracking error while maintaining stable motion.

This controller is trained with PPO under domain randomization~\citep{schulman2017,tobin2017}. Let $\bar{u}_t^{\mathrm{body}}=[v_{x,t},v_{y,t},\omega_{z,t}]^{\top}$ denote the realized body velocity. The reward combines velocity-tracking, control-smoothness, and posture-stability terms:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
r_t^{\mathrm{low}}
=\,&-\lambda_v \left\| \bar{u}_t^{\mathrm{body}}-u_t^{\mathrm{high}} \right\|_2^2 \\
&-\lambda_u \left\| u_t^{\mathrm{joint}} \right\|_2^2 -\lambda_p \phi_t^{\mathrm{stab}}
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\phi_t^{\mathrm{stab}}$ penalizes unstable motions such as excessive roll, pitch, or slippage. Randomizing dynamics and terrain parameters improves robustness, and the trained $\pi^{\mathrm{low}}$ is frozen before high-level learning.

With this motion interface fixed, the high-level process induces a reduced closed-loop transition kernel $P_{\mathrm{cl}}(\cdot\mid x_t,u_t^{\mathrm{high}})$. For high-level training, this kernel is approximated by a nominal closed-loop map, yielding
\begin{equation}
x_{t+1}=\bar{f}_{\mathrm{cl}}\!\left(x_t,u_t^{\mathrm{high}}\right)
\end{equation}
where $\bar{f}_{\mathrm{cl}}$ denotes the nominal closed-loop abstraction obtained by composing the robot dynamics with the frozen low-level tracker. Domain-randomized rollouts indicate that this induced kernel is substantially more concentrated than the transition distribution produced by end-to-end joint-space control, making the nominal abstraction useful at the navigation level.

Given this fixed interface, the high-level policy solves the navigation problem using the MCRA formulation introduced in Section~2. Let the high-level observation be $o_t \in \mathcal{O}$, which contains local LiDAR-based obstacle information, relative goal information, and the robot's state. The high-level action is the commanded base velocity
\begin{equation}
u_t^{\mathrm{high}}=\pi^{\mathrm{high}}(o_t,z_t)
\end{equation}
where $z_t$ is the remaining budget variable.

The high-level policy is conditioned on the remaining budget $z_t$, so that one policy can adapt to different cost-feasibility levels. Intuitively, larger budgets permit more conservative maneuvers, whereas tighter budgets encourage shorter and more efficient motion, provided that safety is preserved.

The high-level objective is to learn a policy that safely reaches the goal within feasible cost budgets while enabling the search for the minimum feasible initial budget:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\min_{\pi^{\mathrm{high}}, z_0}\quad & z_0 \\
\mathrm{s.t.}\quad &
x_T \in \mathcal{G}, \;
x_t \notin \mathcal{F}, \ \forall t\le T .
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

\noindent\textbf{Assumption 1 (Concentrated closed-loop abstraction).}
With the low-level tracker frozen, the induced high-level closed-loop transition kernel is concentrated around $\bar{f}_{\mathrm{cl}}$. In particular, the one-step state deviation
\(
e_t:=x_{t+1}-\bar{f}_{\mathrm{cl}}(x_t,u_t^{\mathrm{high}})
\)
and the relative cost overrun ratio admit bounded upper quantiles under domain-randomized rollouts.

\subsection{Data-driven Robustness Margins}
% TASK-008：式 (19)–(23) 与 Remark 1

Despite the concentrated closed-loop abstraction, residual mismatch remains during real-world quadruped execution~\citep{smirnova2019,wang2021,liu2022drql}. This mismatch mainly manifests as cost overruns and trajectory deviations, which can respectively violate budget feasibility and reduce safety margins.

To account for this residual mismatch, two conservative margins are introduced: a \emph{cost margin} $\alpha$ for budget robustness and a \emph{safety margin} $\delta$ for geometric robustness.

The cost margin inflates the effective running cost:
\begin{equation}
\tilde{c}^{\mathrm{high}}(x_t,u_t^{\mathrm{high}})
=(1+\alpha)\, c^{\mathrm{high}}(x_t,u_t^{\mathrm{high}})
\end{equation}
so that the high-level budget update becomes
\begin{equation}
z_{t+1}=z_t-\tilde{c}^{\mathrm{high}}(x_t,u_t^{\mathrm{high}})
\end{equation}
This design reserves additional budget against execution-induced cost overruns. The margin $\alpha$ is estimated from the upper quantiles of the empirical cost-overrun distribution:
\begin{equation}
\alpha = Q_{p_c}(\varepsilon_c)
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon_c$ denotes the relative cost overrun ratio between actual and nominal execution.

The safety margin enlarges the unsafe set as~\citep{renganathan2023,theurkauf2023}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{F}_{\delta}=\{x\in\mathcal{X}\mid d(x,\mathcal{F})\le \delta\}
\end{equation}
where $d(x,\mathcal{F})$ denotes the distance from the state to the unsafe set. Replacing $\mathcal{F}$ with $\mathcal{F}_{\delta}$ encourages greater obstacle clearance and improves robustness to trajectory deviations. The margin $\delta$ is estimated from the upper quantiles of the empirical trajectory-deviation distribution:
\begin{equation}
\delta = Q_{p_d}(\varepsilon_d)
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon_d$ denotes the deviation from the nominal body path. Here, $Q_p(\cdot)$ denotes the empirical $p$-quantile. The resulting surrogate problem is solved on the nominal closed-loop abstraction $\bar{f}_{\mathrm{cl}}$ with inflated running cost $\tilde{c}^{\mathrm{high}}$ and enlarged unsafe set $\mathcal{F}_{\delta}$.

\noindent\textbf{Remark 1 (Quantile-based robustness intuition).}
If a nominal trajectory generated by $\bar{f}_{\mathrm{cl}}$ reaches $\mathcal{G}$ while remaining outside $\mathcal{F}_{\delta}$ and satisfying the inflated budget update with $\tilde{c}^{\mathrm{high}}$, then the realized trajectory remains safe and budget-feasible whenever the trajectory deviation and cost overrun stay within the empirical quantile ranges used to define $\delta$ and $\alpha$.

\subsection{Two-phase Solver}
% TASK-008：式 (24)

Under this robustified formulation, the high-level policy is trained using a two-phase RL procedure. In \emph{Phase I}, a stochastic policy $\pi_\theta^{\mathrm{high}}(u\mid o,z)$ is trained together with a reachability critic on the augmented state $\hat{x}_t=(x_t,y_t,z_t)$. On the closed-loop abstraction, the critic is trained with the discounted reachability target
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\tilde{V}_{\psi}(\hat{x}_t)
=\,&(1-\gamma)\hat{g}(\hat{x}_t) \\
&+\,\gamma\, \mathbb{E}\!\left[
\min\!\left\{\hat{g}(\hat{x}_t),\tilde{V}_{\psi}(\hat{x}_{t+1})\right\}
\right]
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{g}(x,y,z)=\max\{g(x),Cy,-z\}$ and $\hat{x}_{t+1}$ is generated by $\bar{f}_{\mathrm{cl}}$. During training, the initial budget $z_0$ is sampled from a prescribed interval $[z_{\min},z_{\max}]$.

In \emph{Phase II}, the stochastic actor is converted into a deterministic deployment policy using its mean action, and the value function is re-evaluated under the uncertainty-aware model defined by $\tilde{c}^{\mathrm{high}}$ and $\mathcal{F}_{\delta}$. The minimum feasible initial budget is then obtained by a one-dimensional search over $z_0$ for the start state or start-state distribution.

We compare HD-MCRA with RA~\citep{hsu2021}, RC-PPO~\citep{so2024}, reward-shaped PPO, and CPPO with two safety weights ($\beta_L$, $\beta_H$). Performance is measured by Success Rate (SR), Collision Rate (CR), Average Cost, Budget Violation Rate (BVR), and Minimum Clearance (MC).

\section{Limitations}

While HD-MCRA improves the trade-off among success, safety, budget feasibility, and efficiency in the considered quadruped navigation tasks, several limitations remain. First, its robustness mechanism is based on a nominal closed-loop abstraction and empirical quantile corrections rather than a strict stochastic MCRA guarantee. As a result, the conservative margins $\alpha$ and $\delta$ may become insufficient when the execution distribution deviates substantially from the domain-randomized training conditions. Second, the current system focuses on navigation policy design and relies on LiDAR-based observations, which may provide limited geometric saliency for thin structures such as chair legs, especially under partial occlusion or small clearances. Future work will explore more principled stochastic reachability formulations, uncertainty-aware adaptive safety margins, and stronger multimodal perception for geometry-sensitive safety.

\section{Conclusion}

In this paper, we formulated quadruped navigation as an MCRA problem and proposed HD-MCRA, which combines a frozen low-level tracker, a nominal closed-loop abstraction, a budget-conditioned high-level policy, and conservative cost and safety corrections. This design makes augmented-state MCRA practical for quadruped navigation and achieves a better trade-off among success, safety, budget feasibility, and efficiency than the evaluated baselines.
